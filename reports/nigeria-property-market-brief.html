Summary

Demand is deep, but it sits below where listings are priced.

Asking prices and household budgets have moved apart, and almost everything on the market is priced in two cities. Six findings frame the rest of this brief.

Two cities set the market

Lagos and Abuja hold 98% of rent listings and 95% of sale listings in the index.

National medians are, in practice, Lagos and Abuja medians.

Budgets trail asking

Typical rent budgets sit 66% below asking and sale budgets 45% below. Only 18% of rent listings fall inside the typical rent budget.

Gap is widest for larger flats and for prime Lagos Island and Apapa stock.

The mix is wrong

Searches skew to 1 and 2 bedrooms (+20 and +14 points over supply). Homes of 4 bedrooms and above are over-listed by about 18 points each.

1-bedroom homes are 3 in 10 alerts but 1 in 10 listings.

Lets are slowing, sales are quickening

Median time to let rose from 20 days (Q1 2025) to 28 days. Median time to sell fell from 118 days (Q1 2026) to 94.

Based on listings advertisers marked as let or sold.

Diaspora share is small and steady

6.6% of enquiries come from abroad (US 37%, UK 29%). Interest is stronger for sale and land than for rent.

Share is far higher in Oyo, Enugu and Delta (23% to 28%).

Most series are new

The price index is a reconstructed September 2026 baseline, demand search data starts 17 Sept 2026 and affordability is a first release.

Genuine year-on-year price change arrives from September 2027.

Implications · our reading

Where the data points for decisions.

These are interpretations of the figures above, not statements from the data publisher. Each cites the evidence behind it.

Developers

Build for the unit mix people search for

Demand concentrates in 1 and 2 bedroom homes, while 4 and 5+ bedroom homes are over-listed by about 18 points each. High-pressure rental areas such as Agege, Alimosho, Apapa and Ipaja are where supply is thinnest.

Bedroom mismatch · Agege 72.2 searches per listing
Investors

Test pricing before underwriting prime sale stock

Typical sale budgets sit 45% below asking nationally and 95% below in Lagos Island. In contrast, buyers in Ikoyi, Lekki and Ajah houses budget at or above asking, so depth varies sharply by submarket.

Lagos Island −95% · Ikoyi 4-bed houses +33%
Expansion

Look at secondary cities with unmet demand

Warri, Owerri, Asaba and Awka show high sale search pressure, and Enugu has 55% of rent searches returning nothing. Samples in these states are small, so confirm locally before committing capital.

Warri 22.5 · Enugu 55.1% empty rent searches
Diaspora

Target sale and land, and a few states

Overseas buyers weigh toward sale and land, and toward Oyo, Enugu and Delta. Marketing to the US and UK reaches two-thirds of overseas enquiries.

US 37.0% · UK 29.3% · Oyo land 33.5%
Method and cautions

What the numbers can and cannot support.

Asking prices on one portal, not transactions

Every price is an advertised price from listings on Nigeria Property Centre. Listings skew heavily to Lagos and Abuja, and figures do not reflect agreed or completed prices.

Several series are baselines, not trends

The September 2026 price index is reconstructed from archived listings and the method changed that month, so month-on-month change is unavailable and year-on-year starts September 2027. Demand search data begins 17 Sept 2026. Affordability is a first release. Search-from-abroad figures start in Q1 2027.

Small samples and displayed rows

The source flags states with few listings as small samples and shows only some states per table (for example 11 of 27 sale states). A few cells look like data errors and are noted in context.

Time-to-let counts only homes marked let or sold

The index measures homes that let or sold, not every listing, and excludes 12.2% of first markings (1,756 of 14,361) as bulk markings.

Quarterly market reports not summarised

The market reports page lists a Q4 2026 report (published 3 Oct 2026, 135,391 listings, 46 areas) and a Q3 2026 report (140,501 listings, 47 areas). Both are dated before their quarter ends, and the individual reports were not opened for this brief.
